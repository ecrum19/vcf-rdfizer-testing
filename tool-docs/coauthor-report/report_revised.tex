\section{1. The argument of the paper}\label{the-argument-to-agree-on}

\textbf{VCF-RDFizer provides a consistent route from VCF to an explicit representation that can be reused in linked, policy-aware genomic queries. Its value lies in representation of variant relationships explicetly as well as evaluation of generated representations to ensure consistency, with the cost of conversion, storage, and evaluation measured openly.}

VCF remains compact and effective. Our converter generates a RDF representation of VCF that preserves the VCF information while also providing additional semantic context and capabilities. The paper therefore does not argue that RDF makes these questions possible for the first time, that it is universally faster, or that attaching a policy makes a genome private.

The revised manuscript asks three questions: \textbf{RQ1: what information is preserved and checkable? RQ2: what can the representation do in a linked, policy-aware workflow? RQ3: when are its costs practical?} Results follow that order. The vocabulary is explained through the representation decisions needed to assess the tool, not through a catalogue of classes.

The strongest connection is between the evidence types: source comparisons and structural checks make the transformation inspectable; shared identities make separately maintained information reusable; appropriate baselines and stage timings show what that reuse costs.

\textbf{Needed Feedback:} approve the central claim and scope, decide how to present the validation results, and agree about any remaining work necessary before submission.

\section{2. Validation: the new results change the interpretation}\label{validation-the-new-results-change-the-interpretation}

An \emph{oracle} is the independently derived expected answer against which the graph is checked. SHACL adds constraints on graph structure and values. Neither a query summary nor a shape profile checks everything.

\subsection{What happened in the consumer-genome follow-up?}\label{what-happened-in-the-consumer-genome-follow-up}

Q9 and Q10 expected inventories omitted \textbf{30,910 phase sets} emitted for GATK's \texttt{PS} field. Q11 differed on \textbf{1,998 QUAL values with trailing zeros}, because QLever returned canonical decimals. Direct comparison found the record fields equal to the original VCF text across all 250,000 records; recomputing the expected digest with canonical QUAL reproduced QLever's buckets.

These diagnostics support an oracle/normalization explanation for the three failures. They do not turn the automated run into a pass or prove every emitted relationship. The first shape-enabled attempt exhausted memory; the completed comparison omitted shapes. The evaluated release contains no fix; Section 5 gives the status of one.

\subsection{What matters beyond pass counts?}\label{what-matters-beyond-pass-counts}

Three fixed defects give the validation design a concrete purpose: a header-only input omitted its declared samples; expanded genotypes referenced allele resources missing in raw-INFO mode; and withdrawn files left their headers in release views. Record counts alone could not show any of them. The deeper shape profiles, however, took \textbf{33.7 and 73.2 seconds on roughly 2,000 triples}, versus 1.7 seconds for the default.

\textbf{Reading:} main §§3.1 and 4.2; supplement §§S4 and S8.

Results include all admitted ClinVar classifications with at least one review star. This is not an implementation of all conditions for reporting clinical secondary findings.

\textbf{The frequency join is the clearest integration result.} A separate 68,635-site graph holds the required panel information once: 6.5M triples instead of approximately 857M triples of repeated panel INFO. Shared SPDI links connect genomes, ClinVar and frequencies. The reduction comes from projecting and factoring the input, not from RDF compression.

A rule restricting HFE C282Y to clinical care changes policy data without modifying the RDF policy engine. The baseline needs a small code and data edit. The other three changes are comparably small data edits in both routes. Keep this as a change-location demonstration, not a usability or line-count claim.

\subsection{Important boundaries}\label{important-boundaries}

SPDI joins assume the tested reference, normalization and eligible allele classes. Contig-alias handling is demonstrated; canonical identity across every equivalent representation is not. The whole-genome clinical view releases everything and the other two views nothing. It therefore does not demonstrate selective, non-empty whole-genome masking. All participants are represented as single-sample files, and all experimental consents are simulated. Deployment security, real consent validity and multi-sample masking remain separate questions.

\textbf{Reading:} main §§2.3.2, 3.2 and 4.1; supplement §S5.

\section{4. Cost: compare stages and workloads, not one headline number}\label{cost-compare-stages-and-workloads-not-one-headline-number}

The updated whole-genome use case generates \textbf{662.4M genomic triples}; converting the genome and ClinVar takes \textbf{54 minutes}. Linking takes 8 minutes; the clinical view takes 38 minutes to write and 68 minutes to check; query indexing takes 45 minutes; and the post-index query takes 205--209 seconds. The query index contains \textbf{701.5M triples including ClinVar and links}. These are distinct stages; checking may build an additional index. Peak use is 13.1 GB memory and 34 GB disk.

The separate v3.1.0 corpus workflow takes \textbf{16.07 hours} for a \textbf{657.4M-triple} graph, including about \textbf{14.5 hours building both HDT and COTTAS}. Its reported 10.88 GB sums retained alternatives and an HDT index relative to a 139 MB compressed download. It is not the minimum time or storage required for RDF. Do not merge these configurations or describe the 146.8-second RML stage as complete conversion.

\subsection{What moved out of the main text?}\label{what-moved-out-of-the-main-text}

The main paper now has \textbf{three figures and five tables}. Its figures explain the representation/workflow, sample-granularity trade-off and retrieval comparison. Its tables carry the representation contract, validation evidence, matched outputs, stage costs and indexed regional baseline.

The supplement retains the full converter and capability matrices, detailed semantic model, chunk-construction algorithm, Q1--Q13 specification, run and input manifests, link-service results, exact line counts, complete scaling/representation results, configuration tests, failures and defect history.
